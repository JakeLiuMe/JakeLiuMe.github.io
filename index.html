Letter to readers

This report covers my 2026: my first months as a banker, and the software I built this year.

In May I joined Citi as a Universal Banker in Midtown Manhattan.⁠4 The work is simple to describe and hard to do well: understand what a client needs, recommend what fits, and follow through.

I also build software. I direct AI coding agents to ship quickly, and I hold their output to written rules: tests, approval gates, and a list of claims they may not make. Before I joined Citi, I spent six weeks shipping a developer API that went on to pass 30,000 npm downloads.⁠1

Each segment review below states the result first, then how I got there and what I would change. Every number carries a note with its source and date, and the risks are listed as plainly as the results. I’m glad to walk through any of it.

04

Risks and controls

What could go wrong, stated as plainly as the results, and the habits I use to control it.

Risk factors

  1. A short banking record

    I have been at Citi since May 2026,⁠4 so my banking record is short.

  2. AI agents write most of my code

    I direct AI coding agents and review their work rather than hand-writing most of it. I make up for it with written specs, tests and release gates, and I say so up front.

  3. Downloads overstate users

    WebPeel’s 30,000+ npm downloads include bots, mirrors and CI runs.⁠1 I read them as reach, not as customers.

How I control them

  1. Every number has a source

    Each figure on this site links to a note with its source and date. If I can’t source a number, it doesn’t appear.

  2. Written rules for AI agents

    My agents work from a verified-facts list and a list of claims they may not make. The step that publishes this site leaves out anything tagged as waiting on an approval.

  3. Employer information stays private

    Client results appear here only as percentages, and only with my employer’s approval. No client details, dollar figures or internal documents.
